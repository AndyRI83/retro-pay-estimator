Regular pay and most paid time

The simple part: what Workday paid before the raise versus the corrected rate.

Plain English

For each week, find how much the hourly rate went up. Multiply that difference by the hours Workday paid at that rate.

retro = hours × (corrected hourly rate − original hourly rate)
Example: 36 hours at Step 12. Original rate $64.20. Corrected rate $66.29.
36 × ($66.29 − $64.20) = $75.24.

Overtime

This is the part that makes a simple 1.5× calculator miss a little money.

Why your OT rate changes from week to week

Extra pay earned that week can push your OT rate up. Night, Weekend, Resource / Flow, Baylor, Preceptor, and some holiday pay can all matter. That is why the checker rebuilds OT one week at a time.

weekly regular rate = base rate + (extra pay that counts ÷ hours that count)

OT rate = base rate + 0.5 × weekly regular rate

Two weeks can have the same base wage and still have different OT rates.

Why does Workday sometimes show a bizarre negative OT “rate”?
On a retro reversal, the number in Workday's Rate column can be an internal payroll calculation piece rather than your literal old OT hourly rate. The checker does not use that number as if it were a normal hourly rate. It uses the dollars Workday reversed and rebuilds the week.

Baylor

What Baylor changes
Baylor pay itself: it is tied to the base wage, so the raise changes the Baylor amount.
Overtime: Baylor dollars can raise the OT rate. The extra Baylor pay hours are not treated as extra hours you physically worked when that weekly rate is built.

Holidays

Workday likes to turn one holiday shift into several lines. Because apparently one line would have been too restful.

Major holiday hours that were not already OT
major holiday rate = 1.5 × base rate

Retro changes that holiday amount when the base wage changes.

Major holiday hours that were already OT

Workday can show the weekly OT payment plus a separate extra half-time holiday line.

extra holiday line = 0.5 × base rate

Step changes

What happens if you moved to the next step during the retro period?
The checker follows the wage rate shown for each payroll week. It does not assume you stayed on one step for the entire year. If the rate changes in a way that matches the next wage step, the report can point that out, but it does not invent an anniversary date that the PDF cannot prove.

Rounding

Why can the answer be off by a few cents?
Workday rounds lots of individual lines to cents. The checker carries more decimal places while doing the math, then compares its result with those posted lines. A few cents across hundreds of lines can be normal rounding. A larger difference is only flagged when the rule is well enough understood to support that warning.
